    2.

    Let \(\vec{a}\text{,}\) \(\vec{b}\) and \(\vec{c}\) be non-zero vectors (\(\mathbf{a}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) in the lecture notes). Is each expression a scalar, a vector, or undefined? Tick one box for each.
    🔗
    Expression Scalar Vector Undefined
    \(\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}\)
    \((\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b})\,\vec{c}\)
    \(\vec{a}\cdot(\vec{b}\cdot\vec{c})\)
    \(|\vec{a}|\,\vec{b}\)
    \(\vec{a}+|\vec{b}|\)
    \((\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b})/|\vec{b}|\)

    3.

    Let \(A=(-4,2,5)\) and \(B=(2,-1,7)\text{.}\) Three proposed unit vectors from \(A\) towards \(B\) are
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \vec{u}=\frac{1}{7}(6,-3,2),\qquad \vec{v}=\frac{1}{49}(6,-3,2),\qquad \vec{w}=-\frac{1}{7}(6,-3,2). \end{equation*}
    Which is correct? Justify your choice and explain what is wrong with each of the others.
    🔗
    🔗

    Practice.

    Combine forces using vector addition.

    4.

    Two forces act on a bracket: \(\vec{F}_1=(6,-8,0)\) N, and \(\vec{F}_2\) has magnitude \(15\) N in the direction \(\vec{v}=(-2,1,2)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the components of \(\vec{F}_2\) and the sum \(\vec{R}=\vec{F}_1+\vec{F}_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the magnitude of \(\vec{R}\) and the unit vector \(\hat{R}\) in its direction.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find the angle between the forces, to the nearest degree.
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Explain in your own words why \(|\vec{R}|<|\vec{F}_1|+|\vec{F}_2|\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Practice (continued).

    Include units in your answers. The unit vectors \(\ihat\text{,}\) \(\jhat\) and \(\khat\) are the bold \(\mathbf{i}\text{,}\) \(\mathbf{j}\) and \(\mathbf{k}\) of the lecture notes.
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A force \(\vec{F}=8\ihat-6\jhat+5\khat\) N moves a slider along a straight rail from \(P=(1,3,-1)\) to \(Q=(3,1,0)\text{,}\) with coordinates in metres.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the displacement \(\vec{d}=\overrightarrow{PQ}\) and the distance the slider moves.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the scalar component and vector projection of \(\vec{F}\) along \(\vec{d}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find \(\vec{F}_\perp=\vec{F}-\vec{F}_\parallel\text{,}\) where \(\vec{F}_\parallel\) is your projection. Check that \(\vec{F}_\perp\cdot\vec{d}=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Calculate the work \(W=\vec{F}\cdot\vec{d}\text{.}\) Check it using part (b). Why does \(\vec{F}_\perp\) do no work?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Challenge: algebra meets geometry.

    Connect the sign of a dot product with the angle between two vectors.

    6.

    For real \(x\text{,}\) let \(\vec{a}=(x,2,1)\) and \(\vec{b}=(x-4,x,-1)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find all values of \(x\) for which \(\vec{a}\) and \(\vec{b}\) are perpendicular.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    For which values of \(x\) is the angle acute? For which is it obtuse?
    🔗
    🔗

    Use completing the square and the dot product together.

    7.

    At noon, boats \(A\) and \(B\) have position vectors \(13\ihat+5\jhat\) and \(2\ihat+3\jhat\) relative to a harbour, in kilometres. They move with constant velocities \(8\ihat+9\jhat\) and \(12\ihat+12\jhat\text{,}\) in kilometres per hour.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the vector \(\vec{d}\) from \(A\) to \(B\) at time \(t\) hours after noon.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    By completing the square in \(|\vec{d}|^2\text{,}\) find when the boats are closest and how far apart they are then.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Show that, at that time, \(\vec{d}\) is perpendicular to the velocity of \(B\) relative to \(A\text{.}\) Explain in your own words why.
