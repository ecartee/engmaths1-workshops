    Worksheet Workshop 3: Determinants, inverses and linear systems
    

    Warm-up.

    Please work in collaboration with your classmates to complete the following exercises. This means sharing ideas and asking each other questions.
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Let
    \begin{equation*} M=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 1\\ 2 & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}, \quad N=\begin{bmatrix}3 & 5\\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(\det M\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(N^{-1}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Suppose that \(A\) and \(B\) are \(3\times 3\) matrices. Decide whether each statement is true or false.
    🔗
    Statement True False
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\det(A^T)=\det A\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\det(A+B)=\det A+\det B\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\det(AB)=\det(BA)\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\det(2A)=2\det A\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\)If the columns of \(A\) are linearly dependent, then \(\det A=0\text{.}\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\)If \(\det A=0\text{,}\) then \(A\) has a row of zeros or two equal rows.
    🔗

    Practice: Triple Products.

    🔗

    3.

    Let \(\vec{v}_1=2\ihat+\jhat-2\khat\) and \(\vec{v}_2=\ihat+2\jhat+2\khat\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Show that \(\vec{v}_1\) and \(\vec{v}_2\) are perpendicular and have the same length.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find a vector \(\vec{v}_3\) such that the parallelepiped with edges \(\vec{v}_1\text{,}\) \(\vec{v}_2\) and \(\vec{v}_3\) is a cube. Is there more than one choice?
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What volume should this cube have? Check your answer using a triple product.
    🔗

    Practice: Inverses.

    🔗

    4.

    The matrix
    \begin{equation*} R(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    rotates vectors anticlockwise through an angle \(\theta\) about the origin.
    🔗

    (a)

    Show that \(\det R(\theta)=1\) for every \(\theta\text{.}\) What does this tell you about areas?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(R(\theta)^{-1}\text{.}\) Explain geometrically why your answer is \(R(-\theta)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Notice that \(R(\theta)^{-1}=R(\theta)^T\text{,}\) so \(R(\theta)^TR(\theta)=I\text{.}\) Suppose that \(U\) is a square matrix with \(U^TU=I\text{.}\) Use the properties of the determinant to show that \(\det U=\pm 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Practice: Linear Systems.

    🔗

    5.

    Let \(N\) be the matrix from Exercise 1, and let \(\vec{b}=\colvec{7\\3}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Solve \(N\vec{x}=\vec{b}\) by row reducing the augmented matrix \([N\,|\,\vec{b}]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Solve \(N\vec{x}=\vec{b}\) again, this time as \(\vec{x}=N^{-1}\vec{b}\text{,}\) using your answer to Exercise 1(b).
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Now solve \(N\vec{x}=\colvec{4\\1}\text{.}\) Which method did you use, and why?
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Explain in your own words why row reduction is the better choice for solving a \(3\times 3\) system (or larger).
    🔗

    Practice: Gaussian Elimination.

    🔗

    6.

    Suppose that a workshop makes frames, gates and panels, using a cutting machine, a welding machine and a paint booth. This week the cutting machine is available for 65 hours, the welding machine for 40 hours and the paint booth for 50 hours. The table shows how many hours each item needs on each machine.
    🔗
    Hours per item Frame Gate Panel
    Cutting 1 2 1
    Welding 2 1 0
    Painting 0 1 2

    (a)

    The workshop wants to use all of these hours exactly. Write this problem as \(M\vec{x}=\vec{b}\text{,}\) where \(M\) is the matrix from Exercise 1. Use your answer to Exercise 1(a) to explain why it has exactly one solution.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Use Gaussian elimination to find how many of each item the workshop should make.
    🔗
    🔗

    Challenge: Locating a Phone.

    🔗

    7.

    Suppose that a phone is 5 km from a mast at \((0,0)\text{,}\) 3 km from a mast at \((1,3)\) and 2 km from a mast at \((6,3)\text{,}\) with coordinates in kilometres. Let the phone be at \((x,y)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    For each mast, write down an equation that \(x\) and \(y\) must satisfy.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Subtract the first equation from each of the other two to get a linear system. Solve it to find the position of the phone.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Explain in your own words why this method fails if the three masts lie on a straight line.
