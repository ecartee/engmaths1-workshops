    3.

    The equation of a straight line can be written in either vector form or in Cartesian form. It can be useful to convert back and forth between these two forms.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write the line
    \begin{equation*} \vec{r}=\colvec{1\\-2\\3}+t\colvec{2\\3\\-1} \end{equation*}
    in Cartesian form.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    A second line has Cartesian equation
    \begin{equation*} \frac{x-1}{3}=\frac{4-y}{2}=z-1. \end{equation*}
    Write down a point on this line and a vector along the line. Use this to write the equation of the line in vector form.

    Practice: Matrix Transformations.

    🔗

    6.

    A linear transformation \(T\) is given by \(T(\vec{x})=A\vec{x}\text{,}\) where \(A\) is a \(3\times 3\) matrix. Find \(A\text{,}\) given that
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} T\left(\colvec{1\\0\\0}\right)=\colvec{1\\2\\0},\qquad T\left(\colvec{1\\1\\0}\right)=\colvec{3\\1\\2},\qquad T\left(\colvec{1\\1\\1}\right)=\colvec{2\\4\\5}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Challenge: italics by matrix.

    Digital fonts store each letter as the coordinates of points on its outline. When a font has no italic version, software often makes a slanted one by applying a linear transformation to those coordinates.
    🔗
    🔗

    7.

    Suppose a simple block font draws the word LIFT as shown on the left. Multiplying the coordinates of every point by a \(2\times 2\) matrix \(M\) gives the italic version on the right.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write down the coordinates of the six corners of the upright L and of the italic L. Which points have not moved?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    The columns of \(M\) are the images of \(\colvec{1\\0}\) and \(\colvec{0\\1}\text{.}\) Use the corners from (a) to find these images, and hence \(M\text{.}\)
