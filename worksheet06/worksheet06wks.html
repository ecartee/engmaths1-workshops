    Analyse: Integration by Parts.

    🔗

    3.

    Here are two ways to start trying to integrate \(\displaystyle\int x\ln(x)\,\dd x\) by parts.
    🔗
    Choice \(u\) \(\dfrac{\dd v}{\dd x}\)
    A \(x\) \(\ln(x)\)
    B \(\ln(x)\) \(x\)

    (a)

    Which choice works? Explain in your own words what goes wrong with the other one.

    🔗

    (b)

    Find the integral.

    Evaluate: Improper Integrals.

    🔗

    4.

    Exercise 1(c) is the improper integral
    \begin{equation*} \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x(x+1)}\,\dd x. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Evaluate this integral.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Two students express \(\dfrac{1}{x(x+1)}\) as partial fractions, then argue as follows.
    🔗
    • Student A: \(\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x}\,\dd x\) and \(\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x+1}\,\dd x\) do not exist, so the integral above does not exist either.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Student B: those two integrals are both infinite, so the integral above is \(\infty-\infty=0\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    Explain in your own words what each student has got wrong.
    🔗
    🔗

    🔗

    Create: Your Own Quadratic.

    🔗

    6.

    Work with a neighbour on this exercise. If you are working alone, use your own quadratic in part (b) and check your answer by differentiating it.
    🔗

    (a)

    Choose whole numbers \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\text{,}\) with \(\beta\ne 0\text{,}\) and keep them to yourself. Find the quadratic \(x^2+bx+c\) whose roots are \(\alpha\pm j\beta\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Swap quadratics with a neighbour. Use Exercise 5(b) to write down the integral of one over their quadratic, then swap back and check each other’s answers.

    Challenge: A Vibrating Beam.

    🔗

    7.

    Suppose that engineers hit a beam to test it, and that it then vibrates with displacement
    \begin{equation*} y(t)=e^{-t}\sin(2t) \end{equation*}
    at time \(t\text{.}\) The factor \(e^{-t}\) makes the vibration fade away over time.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-t}\sin(2t)\,\dd t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Without integrating, explain why the answer to part (a) should be positive.
