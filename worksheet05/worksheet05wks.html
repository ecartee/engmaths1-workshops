    Worksheet Workshop 5: Differentiation and integration
    

    Warm-up.

    Please work in collaboration with your classmates to complete the following exercises. This means sharing ideas and asking each other questions.
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Suppose that \(f\) and \(g\) are differentiable functions with these values.
    🔗
    \(x\) \(f(x)\) \(f'(x)\) \(g(x)\) \(g'(x)\)
    \(1\) \(3\) \(-1\) \(2\) \(6\)
    \(2\) \(1\) \(4\) \(3\) \(-2\)
    \(3\) \(2\) \(5\) \(4\) \(7\)

    (a)

    Find \(p'(2)\text{,}\) where \(p(x)=f(x)\,g(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find \(q'(1)\text{,}\) where \(q(x)=f(x)/g(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find \(h'(1)\text{,}\) where \(h(x)=f(g(x))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Find \(h'(3)\text{,}\) or explain why not.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Share out these statements with your neighbours. Decide whether each is true or false, then compare. Explain in your own words why the false ones are false.
    🔗
    Statement True False
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}f(x)=x^{1/3}\) has a derivative at \(x=0\text{.}\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\)The graph of \(y=x^{1/3}\) has a tangent line at \((0,0)\text{.}\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}f(x)=|x|\) has a derivative at \(x=0\text{.}\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}g(x)=x|x|\) has a derivative at \(x=0\text{.}\)
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\)If \(f\) is even and differentiable, then \(f'\) is odd.
    \(\rule[-0.6em]{0pt}{1.8em}\)If \(f'(a)=0\) and \(f''(a)=0\text{,}\) then \(f\) has a point of inflection at \(x=a\text{.}\)

    Practice: Parametric Curves.

    🔗

    4.

    An oscilloscope in XY mode plots one signal against another. Suppose that it plots the signal \(y=\sin 2t\) against the signal \(x=\sin t\text{,}\) for \(0\le t\le 2\pi\text{.}\) The point \((x,y)\) traces the curve below, which is called a Lissajous figure.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find \(\dfrac{\dd y}{\dd x}\) in terms of \(t\text{.}\) At which points \((x,y)\) is the tangent to the curve horizontal? At which is it vertical?
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    The curve passes through the origin twice. Find the slope of the tangent each time.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Mark your points from part (a) on the figure. Which way round each loop does the point \((x,y)\) travel as \(t\) increases?

    Practice: Implicit Differentiation.

    🔗

    5.

    Without the parameter \(t\text{,}\) the curve in Exercise 4 is described by
    \begin{equation*} y^2=4x^2(1-x^2). \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Use implicit differentiation to find \(\dfrac{\dd y}{\dd x}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Check that your answer agrees with Exercise 4(a) at the point \(\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What happens when you put the origin into your answer to part (a)? Explain in your own words how this fits with Exercise 4(b).
    🔗

    Practice: Cutting a Pie.

    🔗

    6.

    Suppose that three groups each estimate the area of a circular pie of radius \(R\) by cutting it into \(n\) thin pieces. The figure shows how for \(n=6\text{:}\) Group A cuts sectors, Group B cuts rings of equal width, and Group C cuts parallel strips of equal width. Below each pie is its shaded piece, with Group B’s ring cut open and unrolled.
    🔗

    (a)

    For each group, find the area of the dashed triangle or rectangle for a typical piece, and write the group’s estimate as a sum.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Each sum is a Riemann sum. Write down the integral it tends to as \(n\to\infty\text{,}\) without evaluating it.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Explain in your own words the advantages and disadvantages of each group’s method.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Challenge: A Freefall Ride.

    🔗

    7.

    Suppose that a freefall ride at a theme park lifts its riders to the top of a tower and drops them. Let \(h\) be their height above the ground in metres and \(t\) the time in seconds since they were dropped, with upwards positive. For three seconds the riders fall freely, with acceleration \(-10\text{.}\) Then magnetic brakes give them acceleration \(20\) until they stop.
    🔗

    (a)

    Integrate to find the velocity \(v(t)\) from \(t=0\) until the riders stop. When do they stop? Sketch the graphs of \(a\) and \(v\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Suppose that the riders stop just as they reach the ground. How tall is the tower?
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Riders feel a jolt when the brakes come on. Which of \(a\text{,}\) \(v\) and \(h\) are continuous at that moment? Which are differentiable there? Explain in your own words how integrating changes a function.
