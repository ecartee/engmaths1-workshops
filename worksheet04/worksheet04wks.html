    Worksheet Workshop 4: Linear systems, rank and inverses
    

    Warm-up.

    Please work in collaboration with your classmates to complete the following exercises. This means sharing ideas and asking each other questions.
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Let
    \begin{equation*} D=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 2\\ 1 & 4 & 3\\ 1 & 2 & \alpha\end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
    where \(\alpha\) is a constant. Find \(\det D\) in terms of \(\alpha\text{.}\) For which values of \(\alpha\) is \(D\) invertible?
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Each row of the table gives the numbers of equations and unknowns in a linear system. Each column describes its solutions: none, exactly one, a line or a plane.
    🔗
    None One Line Plane
    \(\rule[-0.5em]{0pt}{1.6em}\)2 equations, 2 unknowns
    \(\rule[-0.5em]{0pt}{1.6em}\)2 equations, 3 unknowns
    \(\rule[-0.5em]{0pt}{1.6em}\)3 equations, 2 unknowns
    \(\rule[-0.5em]{0pt}{1.6em}\)3 equations, 3 unknowns

    (a)

    Share out the rows between you and your neighbours. Put a tick in each cell where such a system can exist, and a cross where it cannot. Then compare your answers.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Explain in your own words why a system of 2 equations in 3 unknowns can never have exactly one solution.

    Practice: Balancing Chemical Equations.

    🔗

    5.

    Making nitric acid starts with this reaction between ammonia and oxygen:
    \begin{equation*} a\,\mathrm{NH_3}+b\,\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow c\,\mathrm{NO}+d\,\mathrm{H_2O}. \end{equation*}
    Balancing it means finding positive whole numbers \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) \(c\) and \(d\) that give the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write down one equation for each of nitrogen, hydrogen and oxygen.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Row reduce the augmented matrix. Find the rank of the coefficient matrix and the number of free parameters.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find the balanced equation with the smallest possible whole numbers. Explain in your own words what the other solutions mean chemically.
    🔗

    Challenge: Traffic Flow.

    🔗

    6.

    Suppose that the figure shows the number of cars per hour entering and leaving a network of one-way roads, with \(x_1\text{,}\) …, \(x_5\) cars per hour on the roads between its junctions. Every car that enters a junction must leave it.
    🔗

    (a)

    Explain why \(x_1\text{,}\) …, \(x_5\) satisfy the linear system
    \begin{align*} x_1-x_2 \amp= 500\\ x_1+x_3-x_4 \amp= 550\\ x_2+x_3+x_5 \amp= 250\\ x_4+x_5 \amp= 200. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Row reduce the augmented matrix of this system and write down its general solution. How many free parameters are there? Why is there a row of zeros?
